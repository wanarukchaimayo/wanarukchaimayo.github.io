Principle 1

AI is the auditor. The researcher is the accountable author.

The boundary

AI may detect weak logic, unclear claims, reporting gaps, and readability issues. It must not invent data, methods, references, or conclusions.

Recent reviews consistently frame GenAI as an assistive scaffold, not a substitute for human judgment [7] [8] [9].

Good use

“Identify weaknesses and explain why they matter.”

“Suggest revision directions without changing meaning.”

Unsafe use

“Write my Discussion for me.”

“Add citations and make it sound publishable.”

Rule: AI suggests → Human judges → Human revises → Human is accountable.
Explain the ethical boundary before copy-paste prompting.

Principle 2

Context windows fill up. Restart deliberately.

Why new chats?

Long conversations can cause context-window problems. The AI may forget earlier constraints, mix up sections, or respond with lower precision.

The safer route is to open a fresh chat for each major section, upload the latest full manuscript, and ask for a structured summary before the next audit.

Principle 3

Overall manuscript and abstract: first impression matters

What editors screen quickly

Initial editorial screening often looks for scope, clear objective, appropriate design, readable structure, and whether the manuscript’s sections align. Critical appraisal also examines validity, reliability, applicability, and generalisability [2].

Abstract principle

The abstract should be a reliable miniature of the study: objective, methods, key results, and conclusion. It should communicate novelty without exaggeration.

Structured reporting improves clarity and helps reviewers locate key information faster [1] [2].

Principle 5

Methods: transparency, reproducibility, and reviewer trust

Methods are the credibility engine

The Methods section enables reviewers to judge validity and replicability. It should cover study question, design, population, sample size, outcome measures, statistical analysis, and ethics approval [2].

Reporting guideline ≠ study quality

CONSORT, STROBE, PRISMA, STARD and related tools improve clarity and completeness of reporting, but they do not repair a flawed study design [3] [4].

Principle 6

Results: report what was found — not why it happened

The Results section answers the study questions

Results should correspond to objectives and present findings clearly. The text should highlight key results and point readers to tables/figures without repeating every number [1].

Statistical reporting

Report effect sizes and confidence intervals where appropriate. A non-significant p-value is not proof of “no difference”; conclusions should reflect uncertainty and clinical relevance [3].

Good Results sentence

Reports the direction, magnitude, uncertainty, and table/figure reference.

Bad Results sentence

Explains causes, implications, or mechanisms. That belongs in Discussion.

Teach Results principle.

Principle 7

Tables and figures: AI can preview communication, not verify truth

Use AI carefully

AI can help check whether captions are self-contained, abbreviations are defined, and figures/tables are readable. It must not be trusted to verify numerical accuracy.

Manual final checks

  • Units and axis labels
  • Percentage totals
  • Decimal places
  • Resolution ≥ 300 dpi if required
  • Color accessibility
  • Consistency with Results text
Important: This deck intentionally treats table/figure checking as author responsibility. AI is only a preliminary communication auditor.

Principle 8

Discussion: answer the “So what?” question

Discussion is interpretation, not repetition

A strong Discussion summarizes the principal findings, compares them with prior literature, explains unexpected findings, states limitations, and clarifies contribution [1] [2].

What did we find?
→
How does it compare?
→
What does it mean?
→
What are the limits?
→
Why now?
Guardrail: The Discussion must not claim more than the Results and study design can support.

Principle 9

References: AI must not become a citation machine

Why this matters

Generative AI can produce fabricated references and citation errors. Ethical-use papers warn that hallucinations and inaccurate references threaten manuscript integrity [7] [8].

Correct workflow

Use AI only to flag claims that appear unsupported. Then verify literature manually using databases and a reference manager.

Publisher policies vary, but transparency and author accountability are central [5] [6].

Rule: Do not ask AI to create references. Do not paste AI-generated citations into your manuscript.
Teach reference safety principle.

Principle 10

Transparency: disclose AI use and keep human accountability

AI disclosure is now part of publication ethics

A BMJ bibliometric analysis found that many top journals and publishers have AI policies, but requirements differ substantially across publishers and journals [5].

Human authors remain responsible

Editorial policy reviews emphasize that AI tools should not be listed as authors and that authors should disclose tool name, purpose, and scope of AI use where required [6].

Use disclosure to build trust: tool used + purpose + sections affected + human verification.
